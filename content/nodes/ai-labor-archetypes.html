The Two Archetypes of AI Training Labor

The human stories behind AI training work reveal a labor market divided by stark inequalities and divergent experiences.

Data Labeling and Content Moderation

  • Investigations describe workers—often in the Global South—spending up to 20-hour days sorting through graphic or hateful content to train safety systems.
  • An Oxford study characterizes these AI gig platforms as "digital sweatshops" where psychological distress is common and workers are systematically denied basic labor protections.

Domain Expert Evaluators

  • A newer archetype involves highly educated contractors evaluating model outputs in specialized domains like medicine, law, or engineering.
  • These RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) workers often face "hellish" workflows with multi-tier review structures, only to see jobs later outsourced overseas.
  • Workers frequently report frustration regarding pay, transparency, and job security, underscoring how opaque and fragmented the AI training labor market remains.
